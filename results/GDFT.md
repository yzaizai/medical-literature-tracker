## 4. Clinical efficacy and outcomes of enhanced recovery after surgery for laparoscopic cervical cancer.

- **PMID**: [42741282](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42741282/)
- **Journal**: Biomedical reports
- **Publication Date**: 2026 Oct
- **Authors**: He Wuqi, Luo Tingjuan, Qin Chuming, Liu Xianglin, Lu Shuchang et al.
- **DOI**: https://doi.org/10.3892/br.2026.2193

### Abstract

To evaluate the effects of enhanced recovery after surgery (ERAS) protocols in laparoscopic cervical cancer surgery. The present study included 230 patients with early stage cervical cancer undergoing laparoscopic surgery, divided into the ERAS (n=103) and non-ERAS (n=127) groups. ERAS interventions included pre-admission education, nutritional optimization, effective pain management, early mobilization and goal-directed fluid therapy. The primary and secondary outcomes measured were length of hospital stay, surgical site infections, urinary tract infections and postoperative pain levels. The median length of surgery was longer in the ERAS group compared with the non-ERAS group. However, the ERAS group experienced a significantly shorter hospital stay (4.1±3.1 days) compared with the non-ERAS group (5.7±3.9 days; P=0.0005). There was a reduction in the overall complications and postoperative infections, although these differences were not statistically significant. Notably, ERAS protocols significant improved in postoperative pain management, reducing severe pain scores from days 1 to day 3 after surgery. In conclusion, the ERAS protocols shortened hospital stays and improved pain management in laparoscopic cervical cancer surgery, underscoring their role in optimizing surgical care and enhancing patient recovery.

## 5. Postoperative acute kidney injury in thoracic noncardiac surgery: incidence, impact on outcomes, risk and protective factors, and evolving preventive strategies.

- **PMID**: [43033141](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43033141/)
- **Journal**: Current opinion in anaesthesiology
- **Publication Date**: 2026 Nov 17
- **Authors**: Ishikawa Seiji, Kawagoe Izumi
- **DOI**: https://doi.org/10.1097/ACO.0000000000001721

### Abstract

**PURPOSE OF REVIEW**: Postoperative acute kidney injury (AKI) significantly impairs short- and long-term clinical outcomes after thoracic noncardiac surgery, including increased mortality and prolonged hospitalization. This review synthesizes current evidence on the incidence, prognostic impact, risk factors, and evolving preventive strategies for AKI in thoracic surgery. **RECENT FINDINGS**: Intraoperative fluid imbalance and hypotension are robust, modifiable risk factors for AKI. While goal-directed fluid therapy and machine learning-based Acumen Hypotension Prediction Index (Edwards Lifesciences, Irvine, California, USA) monitoring effectively optimize intraoperative hemodynamics and reduce hypotensive episodes, recent clinical trials and meta-analyses indicate they do not directly reduce postoperative AKI rates after general thoracic surgery. However, optimizing drug administration - such as continuous intravenous tacrolimus and shortened vancomycin duration - has demonstrated clinical benefit. Furthermore, evidence outside thoracic surgery demonstrates that the biomarker-guided Kidney Disease: Improving Global Outcomes care bundle significantly decreases moderate-to-severe AKI in high-risk surgical populations, suggesting potential applicability in thoracic surgical care. **SUMMARY**: Given the multifactorial etiology of AKI, single hemodynamic interventions alone are insufficient for prevention. Perioperative care should integrate multimodality strategies, including optimal fluid and pressure management, pharmacological stewardship, and high-risk screening with care bundles. Further studies are needed to establish tailored preventive protocols, particularly for high-risk thoracic procedures such as lung transplantation and pneumonectomy.

## 6. Decision support and automation in goal-directed fluid therapy: current evidence and remaining questions.

- **PMID**: [42982873](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42982873/)
- **Journal**: Journal of anesthesia
- **Publication Date**: 2026 Nov 04
- **Authors**: Herranz-Monge Octavio, Bujeda-Juberías Beatriz, Pérez-Carbonell Ana
- **DOI**: https://doi.org/10.1007/s00540-026-03941-3

### Abstract

Goal-directed fluid therapy (GDFT) individualizes perioperative fluid administration according to dynamic hemodynamic variables, but its implementation remains inconsistent and its impact on patient-centered outcomes is still debated. Decision-support and automated systems have been developed to improve adherence, identify fluid responsiveness more efficiently, and reduce the workload of protocolized hemodynamic management. This structured narrative review summarizes the evidence across four outcome levels: process, surrogate hemodynamic, intermediate physiological, and patient-centered clinical. The most consistent evidence concerns process improvement: decision support increases protocol adherence and improves the proportion of fluid challenges that achieve a predefined stroke volume response, largely on non-randomized evidence. Improvements in surrogate hemodynamic control have also been reported, and selected randomized trials suggest favorable effects on microcirculatory flow and lactate. These findings should not be interpreted as evidence of clinical benefit. Fluid responsiveness does not necessarily indicate a need for fluid administration, and most available studies remain small, single-center, or focused on surrogate outcomes. Evidence that these systems improve postoperative complications, acute kidney injury, length of stay, or mortality is currently lacking. Ongoing multicenter trials should address this gap. Decision support appears to improve how perioperative fluids are administered, but whether it improves what ultimately happens to patients remains unresolved.

- **Authors**: Huo Fei, An Bin, Liang Hansheng, Sun Liang, Xiao Mengmeng et al.
- **DOI**: https://doi.org/10.3389/fsurg.2026.1901393

### Abstract

Soft tissue sarcomas account for less than 1% of all malignant tumors, of which 10∼15% are located in the retroperitoneum, and liposarcoma constitutes 20% of all soft tissue sarcomas. Of note, retroperitoneal liposarcoma is a rare malignant tumor, with recurrence and asymptomatic characteristics at the initial stage until the liposarcoma is large enough to compress abdominal organs. Those larger than 20 kg, known as "giant liposarcomas," are extremely rare. Most retroperitoneal liposarcoma represents expansive growth, and lymph node metastasis or distant metastasis is rarely reported, making surgical resection as the most effective treatment. Because giant retroperitoneal liposarcomas can cause substantial compression of abdominal and retroperitoneal structures, perioperative management may be complicated by impaired venous return, respiratory compromise, hemodynamic instability, difficult tumor dissection, and major blood loss. Current consensus emphasizes management of primary retroperitoneal sarcoma in experienced multidisciplinary centers. In addition, recent perioperative literature highlights the importance of individualized preoperative optimization, invasive hemodynamic monitoring, goal-directed fluid therapy, adequate blood-product preparation, and structured postoperative care. In this context, we reported a case of a giant retroperitoneal liposarcoma with a diameter 52 cm× 38 cm×30 cm and a weight of 31 kg. Through multidisciplinary team (MDT) consultation, the tumor compressing the surrounding organs was successfully removed.

## 10. Comparison of systemic vascular resistance index-guided versus conventional fluid management on airway pressure, oxygenation and postoperative recovery in patients undergoing one-lung ventilation for thoracic surgery.

- **PMID**: [42769557](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42769557/)
- **Journal**: American journal of translational research
- **Publication Date**: 2026
- **Authors**: Qiu Rui, He Na, He Wei
- **DOI**: https://doi.org/10.62347/DAUL3237

### Abstract

**OBJECTIVE**: To compare the effects of systemic vascular resistance index (SVRI)-guided goal-directed fluid therapy (GDFT) versus conventional fluid management on perioperative airway pressure, oxygenation, and postoperative recovery in thoracic surgery patients with one-lung ventilation (OLV), and to identify independent risk factors for postoperative pulmonary complications (PPCs). **METHODS**: A total of 386 patients were retrospectively enrolled and randomly split into a training set (n=270) and a validation set (n=116) at a 7:3 ratio. The training set was divided into SVRI group (n=155) and control group (n=115). Baseline data, airway pressure, oxygenation, recovery indicators, and 7-day PPCs were collected. Independent risk factors were screened by logistic regression; a nomogram was constructed and evaluated by ROC and calibration curves. **RESULTS**: Baseline characteristics were comparable between the groups (P>0.05). The SVRI group had lower airway pressure, fluid volume, infusion rate, recovery time, and PPCs incidence, as well as better oxygenation and urine output (all P<0.05). Ppeak ≥26.845 cmH2O, Pplat ≥21 cmH2O, ΔP ≥14.945 cmH2O, and PaO2/FiO2 <303.815 mmHg were independent risk factors for PPCs. The combined four-index model showed better predictive efficiency. **CONCLUSION**: SVRI-guided GDFT improves perioperative outcomes and reduces PPCs in OLV patients. The four indicators effectively predict PPCs, and their combined detection enhances risk stratification accuracy.
