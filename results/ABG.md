## 1. Pseudo-Metabolic Alkalosis: A Hidden Laboratory Pitfall With Elevated LDH.

- **PMID**: [43004975](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43004975/)
- **Journal**: The Yale journal of biology and medicine
- **Publication Date**: 2026 Sep
- **Authors**: Patel Tulsi, Wortman Kevin, Merchant Nabil
- **DOI**: https://doi.org/10.59249/YYDV4641

### Abstract

Metabolic alkalosis is characterized by elevated serum bicarbonate and systemic alkalemia. However, not all apparent bicarbonate elevations represent true metabolic disturbances. Severe vitamin B₁₂ deficiency, through its association with markedly increased lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) levels, can cause analytic interference in bicarbonate assays, leading to pseudohyperbicarbonatemia. # Case A 33-year-old man presented with progressive exertional dyspnea, syncope, and paresthesia. Laboratory evaluation revealed pancytopenia, macrocytosis, LDH >3600 U/L, low haptoglobin, and vitamin B₁₂ <68 pg/mL, consistent with pernicious anemia. Despite repeated serum bicarbonate values >45 mmol/L on chemistry panels, arterial and venous blood gases demonstrated normal pH and bicarbonate levels, excluding true metabolic alkalosis. The discrepancy was attributed to assay interference from extreme LDH activity. # Discussion Routine chemistry analyzers measure bicarbonate via NADH-dependent enzymatic spectrophotometry. Markedly elevated LDH can accelerate NADH oxidation, producing falsely high bicarbonate values. In contrast, blood gas-derived bicarbonate, calculated from pH and pCO₂, remains accurate. Recognizing this interference prevents misinterpretation of acid-base disorders and inappropriate treatment. # Conclusion This case underscores pseudohyperbicarbonatemia as an important diagnostic pitfall in patients with markedly elevated LDH, such as those with severe vitamin B₁₂ deficiency. Clinicians should confirm unexpected metabolic alkalosis with blood gas analysis and interpret laboratory results in clinical context to avoid unnecessary intervention.

## 2. Long-term survival with congenital Bartter syndrome: A case report.

- **PMID**: [42989668](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42989668/)
- **Journal**: JPMA. The Journal of the Pakistan Medical Association
- **Publication Date**: 2026 Sep
- **Authors**: Billoo Ghaffar, Ahmed Mubashir, Ullah Khalid, Haq Rabia, Memon Asadullah et al.
- **DOI**: https://doi.org/10.47391/JPMA.31248

### Abstract

Bartter syndrome is a rare congenital salt-wasting renal tubular disorder, caused by defective salt reabsorption in the thick ascending limb of the loop of Henle. It is characterized by hypokalaemia, hyponatraemia, hypochloraemia, metabolic alkalosis, hyper-reninaemia, and hyperaldosteronism. The case of a 26 years old male, resident of Karachi, Pakistan, survivor of congenital Bartter Syndrome, with regular quarterly follow up is presented. At the age of 3 years, he got severe vomiting and diarrhoea and developed hypovolaemic shock. He was investigated and clinically diagnosed with Bartter Syndrome based on serum electrolytes imbalance (hypokalaemia/ hyponatraemia/ hypochloraemia), arterial blood gas analysis (metabolic alkalosis) and raised serum renin and aldosterone levels. Urine analysis also showed elevated sodium, potassium, chloride, calcium, and prostaglandin E2 excretion. After diagnosis, the patient was managed with oral indomethacin and potassium supplements during follow up visits. This is the first reported case of a long-term survivor of congenital Bartter syndrome from Pakistan.

## 3. Severe Hypercapnia and Hemodynamic Instability During Carbon Dioxide Insufflation During Robot-Assisted Thoracic Surgery.

- **PMID**: [42960055](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42960055/)
- **Journal**: Cureus
- **Publication Date**: 2026 Sep
- **Authors**: Watanabe Mototsugu, Shiraha Noriaki, Shikatani Yoshinobu, Uehara Kenji
- **DOI**: https://doi.org/10.7759/cureus.117142

### Abstract

The carbon dioxide (CO2) insufflation system enhances robot-assisted thoracoscopic surgery (RATS) by maintaining a stable capnothorax and facilitating smoke evacuation. However, CO2 insufflation can cause hypercapnia and hemodynamic instability, requiring vigilant monitoring. We report a case examining the severity and time course of CO2-induced hypercapnia during capnothorax-assisted robot-assisted thoracoscopic surgery (RATS). A 68-year-old obese man underwent robot-assisted lung resection with capnothorax, resulting in elevated CO2 levels that caused atrial fibrillation and hypotension. Blood gas analysis showed severe acidosis and hypercapnia. Following discontinuation of the capnothorax, hypercapnia improved within 37 minutes. The surgery was completed successfully without capnothorax, and the patient was discharged on postoperative day 6 without complications. While CO2 insufflation supports minimally invasive surgery, this case documents an extreme hypercapnic event with an arterial partial pressure of carbon dioxide (PaCO2) of 110 mmHg with rapid cardiovascular compromise in a high-risk patient using the capnothorax system, underscoring the need for vigilant monitoring, even with advanced insufflation technology.

## 4. Wearable Sweat Biosystem with Dual Diffusion-Limiting Interface for Dynamic Hyperlactatemia Management in Critical Care.

- **PMID**: [42937563](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42937563/)
- **Journal**: ACS nano
- **Publication Date**: 2026 Oct 27
- **Authors**: Long Yunxiang, Pan Xiaosen, Chen Yao, Li Nanpei, Wang Wentong et al.
- **DOI**: https://doi.org/10.1021/acsnano.6c12805

### Abstract

Hyperlactatemia is a critical indicator of tissue hypoperfusion, metabolic dysfunction, and an adverse prognosis in critically ill patients. Yet conventional monitoring depends entirely on intermittent, invasive blood sampling, which cannot capture the dynamic metabolic fluctuations required to guide timely clinical interventions. Herein, we report a wearable sweat-sensing device built on a dual-diffusion-limiting architecture for the continuous and accurate assessment of clinical hyperlactatemia. This system synergistically modulates lactate diffusion in sweat through a silane-network-encapsulated lactate oxidase layer coupled to a polyvinyl chloride layer. This design effectively delays enzyme saturation, extending the linear detection range for sweat lactate to 0-60 mM and thereby fulfilling the stringent requirements of critical care monitoring. The device also integrates real-time pH and temperature calibration to safeguard measurement accuracy in complex physiological environments. Moreover, linear mixed-effects analysis of 82 paired samples from 33 critically ill patients demonstrated a strong association between sweat and blood lactate levels, with a marginal R2 of 0.840. Sweat lactate effectively identified hyperlactatemia and predicted in-hospital mortality, yielding areas under the receiver operating characteristic curve values of 0.943 and 0.785, respectively. The integrated iontophoresis-microfluidics module further ensures high consistency with bedside arterial blood gas analysis, as validated by a marginal R2 of 0.811. Together, these results establish sweat biosensors as a promising platform for continuous metabolic monitoring, resuscitation assessment, and objective prognostic stratification in critical care settings.

## 6. Unexpected Position of a Hemodialysis Catheter.

- **PMID**: [43001191](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43001191/)
- **Journal**: Cureus
- **Publication Date**: 2026 Oct
- **Authors**: Figueiredo Martins João, Domingos Ana Sofia, Gaião Santos Luís, Guimarães Paula
- **DOI**: https://doi.org/10.7759/cureus.117676

### Abstract

Central venous catheterization is frequently employed to obtain urgent hemodialysis access, and catheter malposition due to unrecognized venous anomalies is an underappreciated complication. We describe the case of a 77-year-old man with end-stage renal disease and an extensive vascular access history who required a temporary hemodialysis catheter after thrombosis of his arteriovenous graft. The catheter was placed uneventfully in the left internal jugular vein under ultrasound guidance, but control radiography showed its tip lying in the left hemithorax, and blood gas analysis from the catheter was compatible with arterial blood. Contrast-enhanced computed tomography revealed the catheter tip within the left superior pulmonary vein, which drained into the left brachiocephalic vein, consistent with partial anomalous pulmonary venous return, a rare congenital anomaly usually discovered incidentally. The catheter was removed without complications, and the patient was managed without hemodialysis until his arteriovenous graft was successfully salvaged two days later. This case highlights that an atypical catheter trajectory combined with an arterialized blood gas does not imply arterial puncture and that anomalous venous anatomy should be in the differential diagnosis.

## 8. Starvation-associated high-anion gap metabolic acidosis following short-term oral semaglutide use: A case report.

- **PMID**: [42948992](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42948992/)
- **Journal**: The Journal of international medical research
- **Publication Date**: 2026 Oct
- **Authors**: Salamat Alaa, AlMatrood Ameen M, Ammar Hani M, Baatiah Nada Y, Elsaban Elrasheed et al.
- **DOI**: https://doi.org/10.1177/03000605261471333

### Abstract

Semaglutide, a glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonist, is increasingly used for weight reduction beyond its approved indications. We report a case of starvation-associated euglycemic ketoacidosis in a previously healthy, nondiabetic young woman following short-term oral semaglutide use. A young adult woman presented with persistent nausea, vomiting, and markedly reduced oral intake after 5 days of oral semaglutide prescribed for weight reduction despite having a normal body mass index. Arterial blood gas analysis demonstrated a high-anion gap metabolic acidosis with ketonemia and ketonuria. Serum glucose and lactate levels were within normal limits. Urine studies showed high sodium excretion and evidence of phosphate and magnesium wasting. Semaglutide was discontinued, and the patient was treated with intravenous fluids, dextrose supplementation, and electrolyte replacement. The acid-base status normalized within 3 days, although electrolyte abnormalities required continued monitoring. This case suggests a possible association between semaglutide use and starvation-associated euglycemic ketoacidosis in nondiabetic individuals, likely mediated by reduced caloric intake and gastrointestinal intolerance of food. Increased clinical awareness is warranted, particularly in patients using glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonists for off-label indications such as weight loss.
