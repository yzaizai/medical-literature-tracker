### Abstract

**OBJECTIVES**: Venous blood gas (VBG) analysis has gained increasing popularity as an alternative to arterial blood gas (ABG) analysis, owing to its lower invasiveness and greater feasibility of sample collection. ## 2. Pseudo-Metabolic Alkalosis: A Hidden Laboratory Pitfall With Elevated LDH.

- **PMID**: [43004975](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43004975/)
- **Journal**: The Yale journal of biology and medicine
- **Publication Date**: 2026 Sep
- **Authors**: Patel Tulsi, Wortman Kevin, Merchant Nabil
- **DOI**: https://doi.org/10.59249/YYDV4641

### Abstract

Metabolic alkalosis is characterized by elevated serum bicarbonate and systemic alkalemia. However, not all apparent bicarbonate elevations represent true metabolic disturbances. Severe vitamin B₁₂ deficiency, through its association with markedly increased lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) levels, can cause analytic interference in bicarbonate assays, leading to pseudohyperbicarbonatemia. # Case A 33-year-old man presented with progressive exertional dyspnea, syncope, and paresthesia. Laboratory evaluation revealed pancytopenia, macrocytosis, LDH >3600 U/L, low haptoglobin, and vitamin B₁₂ <68 pg/mL, consistent with pernicious anemia. Despite repeated serum bicarbonate values >45 mmol/L on chemistry panels, arterial and venous blood gases demonstrated normal pH and bicarbonate levels, excluding true metabolic alkalosis. The discrepancy was attributed to assay interference from extreme LDH activity. # Discussion Routine chemistry analyzers measure bicarbonate via NADH-dependent enzymatic spectrophotometry. Markedly elevated LDH can accelerate NADH oxidation, producing falsely high bicarbonate values. In contrast, blood gas-derived bicarbonate, calculated from pH and pCO₂, remains accurate. Recognizing this interference prevents misinterpretation of acid-base disorders and inappropriate treatment. # Conclusion This case underscores pseudohyperbicarbonatemia as an important diagnostic pitfall in patients with markedly elevated LDH, such as those with severe vitamin B₁₂ deficiency. Clinicians should confirm unexpected metabolic alkalosis with blood gas analysis and interpret laboratory results in clinical context to avoid unnecessary intervention.

## 3. Long-term survival with congenital Bartter syndrome: A case report.

- **PMID**: [42989668](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42989668/)
- **Journal**: JPMA. The Journal of the Pakistan Medical Association
- **Publication Date**: 2026 Sep
- **Authors**: Billoo Ghaffar, Ahmed Mubashir, Ullah Khalid, Haq Rabia, Memon Asadullah et al.
- **DOI**: https://doi.org/10.47391/JPMA.31248

### Abstract

Bartter syndrome is a rare congenital salt-wasting renal tubular disorder, caused by defective salt reabsorption in the thick ascending limb of the loop of Henle. It is characterized by hypokalaemia, hyponatraemia, hypochloraemia, metabolic alkalosis, hyper-reninaemia, and hyperaldosteronism. The case of a 26 years old male, resident of Karachi, Pakistan, survivor of congenital Bartter Syndrome, with regular quarterly follow up is presented. At the age of 3 years, he got severe vomiting and diarrhoea and developed hypovolaemic shock. He was investigated and clinically diagnosed with Bartter Syndrome based on serum electrolytes imbalance (hypokalaemia/ hyponatraemia/ hypochloraemia), arterial blood gas analysis (metabolic alkalosis) and raised serum renin and aldosterone levels. Urine analysis also showed elevated sodium, potassium, chloride, calcium, and prostaglandin E2 excretion. After diagnosis, the patient was managed with oral indomethacin and potassium supplements during follow up visits. This is the first reported case of a long-term survivor of congenital Bartter syndrome from Pakistan.

## 4. Effect modification by arterial pH on the association between extracorporeal cardiopulmonary resuscitation and neurological outcomes after out-of-hospital cardiac arrest with shockable rhythm: A multicenter registry study.

- **PMID**: [43019616](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43019616/)
- **Journal**: The American journal of emergency medicine
- **Publication Date**: 2026 Oct 30
- **Authors**: Park Hang A, Park Juok, Hwang Sung Yeon, Yoon Hee, Lee Gun Tak et al.
- **DOI**: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ajem.2026.10.013

### Abstract

**BACKGROUND**: Arterial pH on emergency department (ED) arrival predicts outcomes after out-of-hospital cardiac arrest (OHCA), but its effect on the association between extracorporeal cardiopulmonary resuscitation (ECPR) and neurological outcomes is unclear. We evaluated this association in patients with an initial shockable rhythm and assessed whether pH modified it. **METHODS**: We retrospectively analyzed the Korean Cardiac Arrest Research Consortium registry. Adults with presumed cardiac-origin OHCA, an initial shockable rhythm, ED cardiopulmonary resuscitation, and arterial blood gas data measured after ED arrival were included. Exposure was ECPR initiated in the ED; the primary outcome was favorable neurological outcome at discharge (Cerebral Performance Category 1-2). Arterial pH was categorized as low (≤6.85), medium (>6.85 and <7.05), or high (≥7.05). Multivariable logistic regression assessed ECPR-by-pH interaction, with optimal 1:2 propensity score matching as a sensitivity analysis. **RESULTS**: Of 2183 patients, 213 (9.8%) received ECPR and 154 (7.1%) had a favorable neurological outcome. The association between ECPR and favorable outcome differed across pH categories (p for interaction = 0.004): adjusted odds ratio (aOR) 7.34 (95% confidence interval [CI], 2.84-18.98) in the low-pH group, 2.07 (95% CI, 1.06-4.03) in the medium-pH group, and 0.85 (95% CI, 0.37-1.94) in the high-pH group. The propensity score-matched analysis showed a similar pattern (p for interaction = 0.036). **CONCLUSIONS**: Initial arterial pH modified the association between ECPR and neurological outcome after OHCA with an initial shockable rhythm. The association was strongest at low pH and not observed at high pH. Arterial pH may inform ECPR evaluation but should not determine treatment thresholds.

## 5. Acute Hyperkalaemia During General Anaesthesia in Two Cows.

- **PMID**: [43027142](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43027142/)
- **Journal**: Animals : an open access journal from MDPI
- **Publication Date**: 2026 Oct 29
- **Authors**: Sánchez Cristina, Elzahaby Dany, Levionnois Olivier Louis
- **DOI**: https://doi.org/10.3390/ani16213409

### Abstract

An 8-year-old, 600 kg, female Simmental cow and a 6-year-old, 662 kg, pregnant Hérens cow were referred for surgical repair of mandibular symphyseal fractures following trauma in two unrelated events. Pre-anaesthetic blood tests revealed mild hypokalaemia in the Simmental cow and mild hypocalcaemia in the Hérens cow. Anaesthesia was induced with xylazine, ketamine, and lidocaine, and maintained with isoflurane combined with a ketamine constant rate infusion. Throughout most of the procedures, physiological parameters remained stable, although mild hypercapnia and progressive bradycardia were noted. During surgical closure, both cows developed sudden severe bradycardia, followed by ventricular escape beats and short periods of asystole. Arterial blood gas analysis revealed acute severe hyperkalaemia, with potassium concentrations of 6.8 mmol/L in the Simmental cow and 8.66 mmol/L in the Hérens cow. Immediate treatment with a calcium-magnesium-glucose solution was initiated, resulting in rapid improvement in heart rate, restoration of sinus rhythm, and reduction in potassium concentrations. Both cows recovered uneventfully, with normal electrolyte values over the following 72 h. This report describes acute intra-anaesthetic hyperkalaemia in two cattle, including a pregnant animal, occurring without an identifiable predisposing factor. Continuous monitoring and rapid recognition of changes in cardiovascular status and electrolyte concentrations allowed prompt treatment and restoration of normal cardiac rhythm.

## 6. Unexpected Position of a Hemodialysis Catheter.

- **PMID**: [43001191](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43001191/)
- **Journal**: Cureus
- **Publication Date**: 2026 Oct
- **Authors**: Figueiredo Martins João, Domingos Ana Sofia, Gaião Santos Luís, Guimarães Paula
- **DOI**: https://doi.org/10.7759/cureus.117676

### Abstract

Central venous catheterization is frequently employed to obtain urgent hemodialysis access, and catheter malposition due to unrecognized venous anomalies is an underappreciated complication. We describe the case of a 77-year-old man with end-stage renal disease and an extensive vascular access history who required a temporary hemodialysis catheter after thrombosis of his arteriovenous graft. The catheter was placed uneventfully in the left internal jugular vein under ultrasound guidance, but control radiography showed its tip lying in the left hemithorax, and blood gas analysis from the catheter was compatible with arterial blood. This case highlights that an atypical catheter trajectory combined with an arterialized blood gas does not imply arterial puncture and that anomalous venous anatomy should be in the differential diagnosis.

## 7. Arterial blood gas parameters as mortality predictors in critically ill patients admitted from the emergency department: a prospective observational cohort study.

- **PMID**: [43016844](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43016844/)
- **Journal**: Journal of critical care medicine (Universitatea de Medicina si Farmacie din Targu-Mures)
- **Publication Date**: 2026 Oct
- **Authors**: Caliskan Koca, Duru Mehmet
- **DOI**: https://doi.org/10.62838/jccm-2026-0040

### Abstract

**BACKGROUND**: Arterial blood gas (ABG) analysis is rapidly available in emergency and intensive care settings and constitutes a core component of many critical illness scoring systems. Although ABG parameters are incorporated into many multidimensional critical illness scoring systems, their independent contribution to mortality prediction remains uncertain because these variables may change rapidly during the early course of critical illness and resuscitation. This study evaluated the prognostic performance of ABG parameters and validated severity scoring systems for predicting 28-day mortality in critically ill patients admitted from the emergency department (ED) to the intensive care unit (ICU). **METHODS**: In this prospective observational cohort study, 242 adult patients admitted from the ED to the internal medicine ICU between January 2012 and March 2013 were enrolled. Admission ABG parameters (pH, PaCO2, PaO2, and HCO3 -) and severity scores (APACHE II, APACHE IV, and SAPS II) were recorded. The primary outcome was 28-day all-cause mortality. Prognostic discrimination was assessed using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis. **RESULTS**: Overall 28-day mortality was 47.1%. Non-survivors had significantly higher APACHE II, APACHE IV, and SAPS II scores than survivors (all p < 0.001). SAPS II demonstrated the best prognostic performance (AUC 0.720; 95% CI 0.659-0.776), significantly outperforming APACHE II and APACHE IV. In contrast, none of the individual ABG parameters independently predicted 28-day mortality (all p > 0.05). **CONCLUSIONS**: Multidimensional severity scoring systems, particularly SAPS II, provide superior prognostic discrimination compared with isolated ABG parameters in critically ill ED patients. Although ABG analysis remains essential for acute physiologic assessment, isolated blood gas variables appear insufficient as standalone mortality predictors in heterogeneous ICU populations.

- **DOI**: https://doi.org/10.12659/AJCR.954468

### Abstract

BACKGROUND During minimally invasive thoracic surgery with intrathoracic carbon dioxide (CO₂) insufflation, an abrupt rise in end-tidal CO₂ (EtCO₂) alone cannot distinguish true hypercapnia from artifactual or exogenous CO₂ contamination. We report 2 contrasting cases in which the direction of the arterial-to-end-tidal CO₂ gradient, Δ(PaCO₂-EtCO₂), guided differential diagnosis. CASE REPORT Case 1: A 62-year-old man undergoing robot-assisted right lower lobectomy developed an abrupt EtCO₂ rise from 40 to 130 mm Hg immediately after CO2 insufflation, with severe hypotension and bradycardia. Arterial blood gas (ABG) analysis showed pH 6.79, PaCO2 228.0 mm Hg, and Δ(PaCO₂-EtCO₂) +98.0 mm Hg, suggesting a possible contribution of low cardiac output to the widened gradient. Insufflation was stopped, 2-lung ventilation resumed, and surgery completed without re-insufflation. Case 2: A 77-year-old man undergoing thoracoscopic subtotal esophagectomy developed an EtCO₂ rise from 54 to 99 mm Hg while hemodynamically stable. After excluding circuit and sampling-line artifacts, ABG analysis showed pH 7.21, PaCO₂ 72.5 mm Hg, and Δ(PaCO₂-EtCO₂) -26.5 mm Hg, suggesting exogenous CO₂ contamination of expiratory gas rather than systemic retention alone. Surgical exploration confirmed a partial injury to the left main bronchus. CONCLUSIONS During abrupt EtCO₂ surges under 1-lung ventilation with capnothorax, the direction of Δ(PaCO₂-EtCO₂) on prompt ABG analysis may help prioritize the differential diagnosis between systemic retention and exogenous contamination, provided hemodynamics and circuit integrity are concurrently assessed. A markedly positive gradient during shock may indicate systemic CO₂ loading with reduced pulmonary perfusion, whereas a negative gradient after artifact exclusion suggests pleuro-airway communication or peri-device leakage. This hypothesis-generating approach remains unvalidated as a standalone diagnostic tool.

### Abstract

## 19. Clinical, Biochemical, and Electrocardiographic Predictors of Mortality in Acute Aluminum Phosphide Poisoning.

- **PMID**: [42780547](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42780547/)
- **Journal**: Journal of toxicology
- **Publication Date**: 2026
- **Authors**: Deraz Raghda H, Saafan Omar, Sabik Laila M E, Abdulrhman Shymaa M N
- **DOI**: https://doi.org/10.1155/jt/4876498

### Abstract

**BACKGROUND**: Aluminum phosphide (AlP) poisoning is among the deadliest pesticide poisonings, particularly in developing agricultural communities, with multiorgan involvement significantly affecting the poisoned patient's outcome. **OBJECTIVE**: To evaluate the prognostic significance of arterial blood gas (ABG), serum troponin I, cortisol, renin, early ECG abnormalities, and the Simplified Acute Physiology Score II (SAPS II) in predicting the outcome of acute AlP poisoning during the first 24 h postingestion. **METHODS**: A prospective cohort study was conducted on 42 patients with confirmed acute AlP ingestion who met the inclusion criteria at Zagazig University Hospitals. Patients were categorized as survivors or nonsurvivors.
