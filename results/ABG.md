## 1. Reversible bilateral cerebellar ataxia resulting from hypercapnic encephalopathy in an undiagnosed patient with obstructive sleep apnoea.

- **PMID**: [42805615](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42805615/)
- **Journal**: BMJ case reports
- **Publication Date**: 2026 Sep 28
- **Authors**: Jain Arihant, Jamshed Nayer, Maheriya Falgun
- **DOI**: https://doi.org/10.1136/bcr-2026-273356

### Abstract

A man in his 40s with morbid obesity and type 2 diabetes mellitus presented with early-morning drowsiness and respiratory distress after several days of nocturnal dyspnoea and unsupervised loop diuretic use. Initial assessment showed hypoglycaemia, acute-on-chronic hypercapnic respiratory acidosis with metabolic alkalosis and obstructive ventilatory mechanics, requiring intubation. Within 24 hours of extubation, he re-presented with recurrent drowsiness and striking bilateral cerebellar ataxia despite normal neuroimaging. Repeat arterial blood gases again demonstrated acute-on-chronic hypercapnic respiratory failure, and his encephalopathy and cerebellar signs resolved completely with ventilatory support and correction of PaCO₂. Subsequent high-resolution CT of the chest showed aspiration-related consolidation without underlying parenchymal lung disease and polysomnography confirmed severe obstructive sleep apnoea. This case illustrates hypercapnic encephalopathy presenting as reversible bilateral cerebellar ataxia in the context of diuretic-associated metabolic alkalosis and suspected chronic CO2 retention and highlights the need for early arterial blood gas analysis and careful interpretation of ventilator waveforms.

## 2. A longitudinal characterization of cardiopulmonary and systemic injury following acute chlorine inhalation in rats.

- **PMID**: [42805570](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42805570/)
- **Journal**: Toxicology and applied pharmacology
- **Publication Date**: 2026 Sep 28
- **Authors**: Jadhav Gajanan R, Johns Amber J, Zafar Iram, Ahmad Aftab, Ahmad Shama
- **DOI**: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.taap.2026.118062

### Abstract

Chlorine is a highly reactive, toxic industrial gas whose accidental or intentional release poses a significant public health threat, highlighting the need for effective medical countermeasures. Well-characterized animal models are essential for understanding the mechanisms of chlorine-induced injury and evaluating potential therapeutics. In this study, we established a longitudinal preclinical rat model of acute chlorine inhalation using two near lethal (LD50) exposures (500 or 600 ppm for 45 min) in unanesthetized adult Sprague Dawley rats. Pulmonary and systemic injury were evaluated at 3, 6 and 24 h postexposure using clinical, biochemical, hematological, physiological and histopathological endpoints. Both exposure concentrations produced approximately 50% mortality with most deaths occurring shortly after exposure, while survivors exhibited severe respiratory distress, increased clinical scores, weight loss, bradycardia and hypoxemia. Arterial blood gas analysis demonstrated impaired gas exchange characterized by hypercapnia and compensated respiratory acidosis. Chlorine exposure induced robust pulmonary inflammation and injury, evidenced by increased BALF protein, LDH and the damage associated molecular pattern (DAMP) HMGB1, pulmonary edema, airway epithelial sloughing, alveolar damage and elevated lung injury scores. Systemic inflammation was characterized by neutrophilia with concurrent lymphopenia and monocytopenia. Vascular injury was indicated by increased syndecan-1 concentrations in BALF and plasma while cardiac involvement was demonstrated by increased heart-to-body weight ratios, elevated circulating cardiac injury biomarkers and ultrastructural myocardial damage. Collectively, this well-characterized model recapitulates the major manifestations of acute chlorine toxicity and provides a robust platform for investigating disease mechanisms and evaluating candidate medical countermeasures.

## 4. Severe alkalemia in the emergency department : Acid-base patterns, clinical presentation and short-term outcomes in hospitalized patients.

- **PMID**: [42850461](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42850461/)
- **Journal**: Wiener klinische Wochenschrift
- **Publication Date**: 2026 Oct 08
- **Authors**: List Philipp, Lindner Gregor, Schwarz Christoph
- **DOI**: https://doi.org/10.1007/s00508-026-02840-7

### Abstract

**INTRODUCTION**: Alkalemia is the most common acid-base disorder in hospitalized patients; however, data on patients with severe alkalemia admitted to the emergency department are limited. This study aimed to characterize the acid-base patterns of patients with severe alkalemia presenting to the emergency department and to describe the clinical presentation and short-term clinical outcomes of those requiring hospitalization. **PATIENTS, MATERIAL AND METHODS**: We conducted a retrospective analysis of arterial and venous blood gas measurements and medical records of all adult nontrauma patients admitted to the emergency department of a tertiary university hospital in Austria with a pH> 7.55, between 1 June 2022, and 31 December 2023. **RESULTS**: Among 215 patients with severe alkalemia, 84 (39%) required hospitalization. Acute respiratory alkalosis was the predominant acid-base disorder (57%), followed by mixed respiratory and metabolic alkalosis (35%), whereas isolated metabolic alkalosis was not observed. Detailed clinical data were available for 80 hospitalized patients. Pain (67%) and emesis (49%) were the most common presenting symptoms, while psychiatric disease was the most prevalent comorbidity (38%). Despite severe alkalemia, the disorder was documented as a diagnosis in only 4% of hospitalized patients. No in-hospital deaths occurred. Application of a published venous-to-arterial correction algorithm resulted in only minor changes in the classification of acid-base disorders. **CONCLUSION**: In contrast to previous reports from intensive care populations, patients with severe alkalemia requiring hospitalization had a clinically distinct phenotype and favorable short-term outcomes. Despite marked alkalemia, the condition was very rarely documented as a diagnosis, suggesting that severe alkalemia might be underrecognized in the emergency department.

## 5. The Impact of Blood Gas Sample Waiting Time on Laboratory Parameters in Intensive Care Patients.

- **PMID**: [42833420](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42833420/)
- **Journal**: Enfermeria clinica
- **Publication Date**: 2026 Oct 05
- **Authors**: Sert Havva, Aydemir Yusuf, Koc Ferhat, Eren Merve Gulbahar
- **DOI**: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.enfcle.2026.502564

### Abstract

**OBJECTIVES**: This study aimed to investigate the impact of blood gas sample waiting time on laboratory parameters in intensive care patients, focusing on pH-related changes. **METHODS**: This prospective longitudinal observational study was conducted in the pulmonary diseases intensive care unit of a training and research hospital in Turkey between July 1 and November 30, 2023. A total of 81 patients were included in the study. Blood gas samples were categorized into three groups based on initial pH levels: acidosis, normal, and alkalosis. The samples were stored at 22 °C and analyzed at 0, 30, and 60 minutes after collection. Statistical analyses were conducted using ANOVA and the Friedman test, and effect sizes were evaluated with Eta squared and Kendall's W. **RESULTS**: Significant time-dependent changes were observed in PaO2 and SaO2 levels in the acidosis group. In the normal pH group, significant changes occurred in pH, PaCO2, PaO2, SaO2, glucose, and lactate levels. Similarly, in the alkalosis group, significant changes were detected in pH, PaCO2, SaO2, HCO3, glucose, and lactate levels. These findings suggested that slower metabolic activity in acidosis samples led to limited changes, whereas earlier changes were observed in normal and alkalosis samples. **CONCLUSIONS**: To ensure accuracy in laboratory parameters, blood gas samples should be analyzed immediately after collection. Future studies with larger sample sizes and diverse methodologies are required to validate these findings and address the study's limitations, if unavoidable, aim for ≤30 min and document delay for interpretation.

## 7. Acute Respiratory Distress Syndrome and Corticosteroids: Reconciling Conflicting Evidence.

- **PMID**: [42845066](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42845066/)
- **Journal**: Acta medica Indonesiana
- **Publication Date**: 2026 Jul
- **Authors**: Tadesse Telila Mesfin, Wakjira Olifan Getachew

### Abstract

Acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS) is a life-threatening form of acute inflammatory lung injury in which loss of alveolar-capillary membrane integrity produces protein-rich pulmonary edema, refractory hypoxemia, and reduced lung compliance, typically within 6-72 hours of a precipitating insult. The 2012 Berlin criteria defined ARDS by acute onset within one week of a known insult, bilateral opacities not fully explained by effusion, atelectasis, or cardiac failure, and a PaO₂/FiO₂ ratio of 300 mmHg or less with a minimum PEEP of 5 cmH₂O. The 2023 global definition expands this framework by accepting SpO₂/FiO₂ of 315 or less when SpO₂ is 97% or below, including patients supported with high-flow nasal oxygen at 30 L/min or more, permitting thoracic ultrasonography as an alternative imaging modality, and waiving the PEEP requirement in resource-limited settings. These revisions are particularly relevant to practice in low- and middle-income countries, where arterial blood gas analysis and mechanical ventilation may be unavailable. Management remains anchored in lung-protective ventilation, conservative fluid strategy, and prone positioning. Glucocorticoids remain the most contested pharmacological intervention: evidence from dexamethasone trials in ARDS and in COVID-19 suggests benefit in selected populations, yet questions persist regarding optimal agent, dose, timing relative to symptom onset, duration, the influence of ARDS subphenotypes, and the risk of secondary infection and neuromuscular weakness. This narrative review summarizes the pathophysiology, evolving diagnostic criteria, imaging findings, and current evidence on corticosteroid therapy, and considers how hyperinflammatory and hypoinflammatory subphenotypes may guide future trial design and individualized treatment.

## 8. Clinical and Inflammatory Benefits of Nebulized Furosemide with Salbutamol-Ipratropium in COPD Exacerbations: A Randomized Controlled Trial.

- **Journal**: Iranian journal of allergy, asthma, and immunology
- **Publication Date**: 2026 Aug 12
- **Authors**: Behi Mahdi, Gholamine Babak, Masjedi Mohammad Reza, Eslaminejad Alireza, Idani Esmaeil et al.
- **DOI**: https://doi.org/10.18502/ijaai.v25is11.22488

### Abstract

Exacerbations of chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) are a leading cause of morbidity, mortality, and healthcare burden. While standard bronchodilator therapy alleviates airway obstruction, its efficacy is often limited. This study evaluated nebulized furosemide, which has bronchodilatory and anti-inflammatory effects, as an adjunct to salbutamol-ipratropium in hospitalized COPD patients. Ninety‑two patients with exacerbated COPD were randomly assigned to two equal groups (46 each). The intervention group received nebulized salbutamol-ipratropium plus furosemide (20 mg), and the control group received salbutamol-ipratropium, both every 8 hours for 10 minutes over 5 consecutive days. Demographic and clinical variables, pulmonary function indices (FEV1, FVC, FEV1/FVC), clinical outcomes (COPD Assessment Test (CAT), Modified Medical Research Council (mMRC)scores, and quality of life by St. George's Respiratory Questionnaire (SGRQ)), arterial blood gases, and inflammatory biomarkers (TNF-α, IL-6, IL-8, CRP, ESR, neutrophil %, WBC) were measured before and after treatment. Demographic characteristics and baseline indices did not differ significantly between the groups. Dyspnea and respiratory symptoms (mMRC and CAT scores) decreased significantly, while quality of life, improved markedly in the intervention group compared to the control group. Pulmonary function also showed significant enhancement, with the FEV₁/FVC ratio increasing. Arterial blood gas analysis showed higher PaO₂ and lower PaCO₂ in the intervention group. IL-6 and IL-8 decreased significantly, and no adverse events were reported. Nebulized furosemide enhances standard therapy in COPD exacerbations, improving lung function, relieving symptoms, and reducing systemic inflammation. Its safety, accessibility, and low cost make it a promising adjunct treatment.
