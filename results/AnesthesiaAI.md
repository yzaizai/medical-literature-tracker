## 3. Multimodality Imaging for Left Atrial Appendage Closure: Practical Guidance Across Preprocedural, Intraprocedural, and Postprocedural Phases.

- **PMID**: [42948875](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42948875/)
- **Journal**: Circulation. Cardiovascular imaging
- **Publication Date**: 2026 Oct 28
- **Authors**: Motairek Issam, Abadie Bryan, Cremer Paul C, Kanj Mohamed, Harb Serge et al.
- **DOI**: https://doi.org/10.1161/CIRCIMAGING.126.020263

### Abstract

Left atrial appendage closure is an increasingly used stroke prevention therapy for patients with atrial fibrillation who are unable to maintain long-term anticoagulation. Imaging quality and consistency are central to procedural safety and long-term outcomes, yet practice varies widely across centers. This review presents a practical, phase-based multimodality imaging framework intended to provide guidance and minimum standards for image acquisition and interpretation across all phases of left atrial appendage closure care, including preprocedural planning and thrombus exclusion, intraprocedural guidance and complication detection, and postprocedural surveillance for device-related thrombus and peridevice leak. Because sizing rules, sealing mechanisms, and leak patterns differ between the 2 contemporary platforms, device-specific considerations for the Watchman and Amulet systems are addressed throughout. Transesophageal echocardiography remains the reference standard for thrombus detection and procedural guidance, while cardiac computed tomography offers high spatial resolution, detailed 3-dimensional characterization, and complementary leak assessment, with optimal modality selection influenced by patient characteristics, operator expertise, and institutional resources. Intracardiac echocardiography and mini transesophageal echocardiographic support anesthesia-sparing workflows. Standardized device-related thrombus and peridevice leak classification and harmonized imaging protocols can enhance reproducibility and facilitate cross-center comparisons. Emerging technologies including computed tomography first workflows, 4-dimensional imaging, AI applications, and computational modeling further advance precision planning and surveillance.

## 4. The Carbon Footprint of Robotic-Assisted Surgery: Lifecycle, Scope-Based Accounting, and Emerging AI Contributions.

- **PMID**: [42947562](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42947562/)
- **Journal**: The American surgeon
- **Publication Date**: 2026 Oct 28
- **Authors**: Patni Suhani, Chao Steven Y, Chen-Goodspeed Amber, Emigh Brent, Robles Jeffrey et al.
- **DOI**: https://doi.org/10.1177/00031348261499829

### Abstract

Robot-assisted surgery (RAS) has become an integral component of modern surgical practice, driven by perceived technical advantages and expanding indications. As these systems become increasingly embedded in routine care, their environmental implications warrant closer examination. The concept of a "carbon footprint" provides a useful framework for understanding the total greenhouse gas emissions associated with surgical care, extending beyond intraoperative energy use to encompass manufacturing, supply chains, anesthesia, and waste. Drawing on lifecycle assessments and comparative studies, this review examines the sources and magnitude of carbon emissions associated with RAS. Reported environmental impacts vary by procedure, institutional setting, instrument reuse, and lifecycle boundaries, with individual studies showing both higher and lower emissions for RAS than laparoscopic or open alternatives. However, a recent GRADE-based systematic review found that RAS had the greatest intraoperative environmental impact among evaluated approaches, although evidence quality was low to very low. Major contributors include single-use instrumentation, operating-room infrastructure, and anesthetic practices rather than the robotic platform alone. The increasing complexity of contemporary robotic systems, including their reliance on advanced instrumentation, digital infrastructure, and high-performance computing, further complicates assessment of embedded and downstream emissions, particularly within poorly characterized Scope 3 categories. At the same time, several contributors to the carbon footprint of RAS appear modifiable through changes in peri-operative practice, procurement strategies, and system design. As robotic surgery continues to expand globally, a clearer understanding of its environmental impact is essential to ensure that technological innovation proceeds in parallel with ethical stewardship and sustainable health-system planning.

- **Publication Date**: 2026 Oct

- **DOI**: https://doi.org/10.17245/jdapm.2026.26.097

### Abstract

**BACKGROUND**: Dental anxiety is prevalent among children worldwide and represents a barrier to pediatric dental care, contributing to care avoidance, treatment difficulty, and poorer oral health outcomes. Venham Clinical Anxiety Scale (VCAS) and Visual Facial Anxiety Scale (VFAS), are limited by subjectivity and interobserver variability. This study evaluated an artificial intelligence (AI)-based facial emotion recognition (FER) application for assessing dental anxiety across procedural stages in children undergoing tooth extraction. **METHODS**: A total of 186 children aged 7-13 years who were undergoing dental extraction were enrolled. EmoDent, a SwiftUI iOS application using a convolutional neural network (CNN) model, captured facial expressions at four time points: pre-treatment, before local anesthesia, during extraction, and post-treatment. VCAS and VFAS scores were recorded. Spearman's rank correlation and Cohen's kappa were applied (P < 0.05). **RESULTS**: Emotional responses varied significantly across treatment phases (P < 0.001). Happy (20.4%) and neutral (58.1%) expressions predominated pre-treatment, shifting to anger (49.5%) and sadness (24.7%) during local anesthesia, with partial recovery post-treatment. Concordance with traditional clinical scales was strongest during peak-stress phases. It offers a promising non-invasive adjunct, particularly for non-verbal children. Future studies should incorporate multimodal data and clinician feedback.

### Abstract

Ultrasound-guided regional anesthesia (UGRA) has emerged as a new technical approach to provide real-time visualization of the needle pathway to minimize the risk of trauma to surrounding structures, notably vessels and nerves. This review will examine the basic concepts of UGRA and describe how regional anesthetic methods are categorized by region. Additionally, it explores technological approaches, offers block-specific insights, and discusses their clinical practice indications, such as postoperative pain management, as well as emergency physicians' use of these techniques to treat severe conditions like fractures, trauma, and muscular injuries, or even as an adequate substitute for procedural sedation. The implementation of UGRA is constrained by institutional hurdles, costs, and skill requirements; consistent training and credentialing are essential to increase proficiency and safety. UGRA promotes healing, reduces opioid use, and improves pain management. Although there are risks, safety and accessibility are enhanced by careful technique, monitoring, and advances such as portable ultrasonography and AI. ## 10. Artificial intelligence and machine learning for prediction of postoperative complications: A systematic review focused on anesthesiology, perioperative risk stratification, and clinical applicability.

- **PMID**: [42960327](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42960327/)
- **Journal**: Saudi journal of anaesthesia
- **Publication Date**: 2026
- **Authors**: Hafiani Hamza, Kassimi Hamza, Bekkour Salma, Bentalba Rim, Elalaa Khalil Abou
- **DOI**: https://doi.org/10.4103/sja.sja_493_26

### Abstract

Artificial intelligence (AI) and machine learning (ML) are increasingly used to predict postoperative complications, but their clinical value for anesthesiologists depends on more than discrimination alone. This systematic review evaluated AI- and ML-based models for predicting postoperative complications in surgical patients, with emphasis on model performance, validation, calibration, timing of predictor availability, interpretability, and clinical applicability. The review followed Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) 2020 principles. Eligible studies developed, validated, or compared AI/ML models for postoperative complication prediction and reported at least one performance metric. Risk of bias was assessed using Prediction model Risk of Bias ASsessment Tool (PROBAST), and reporting quality was interpreted in light of Transparent Reporting of a multivariable prediction model for Individual Prognosis Or Diagnosis (TRIPOD) principles. Thirty-five studies were included. The most frequently predicted outcomes were acute kidney injury, postoperative delirium, cardiovascular complications, infectious complications, and composite postoperative morbidity. Reported best-model discrimination ranged approximately from 0.70 to 0.97. Models incorporating intraoperative variables often showed stronger performance than preoperative-only models, particularly for acute kidney injury and infectious complications, but this reduced their usefulness for early preoperative decision-making. External validation was uncommon, calibration was inconsistently reported, and the analysis domain was the most frequent source of concern in PROBAST assessment. Current AI/ML models for postoperative complication prediction are promising, but their routine use in anesthesiology requires stronger external validation, transparent calibration, workflow integration, and clinically interpretable decision-support strategies.
